The short answer

Most of it, yes. One piece, no, not without AI.

You described seven things. One already works. Three more need nothing but building, with rules you set rather than judgement you cannot check. Two are half and half. One genuinely cannot be done without something that understands what your words mean.

That last one is real and I am not going to soften it. But it is one of seven, not the whole thing, and your own plan always put that decision at the end rather than the start.

Piece by piece

Your words first, then what is actually true about each.

Speaking thoughts in

Works today

"You can speak or type them into a shortcut on your phone."

Done. Double-tap the back of your phone, tap the keyboard microphone, speak, send. Saved word for word with the time on it. Nothing further needed.

Filing thoughts under the right goal

HalfHalf

"The app sections or categorises your thoughts and ideas off into the overall life goal that it aligns with."

With rules you set: yes. You already tag with hashtags. You decide #biz means the automation business and #trading means the trading app, and the app files them accordingly. It can never file something wrongly, because it never guesses.

Working it out for itself: needs AI. Reading "restaurants might have a POS problem" and knowing which goal that serves means understanding the sentence.

Turning an idea into steps

Needs AI

"It gives you steps to completing that thought or idea, therefore you can achieve your goal."

This one has no version without AI, and I will not pretend otherwise. No rule turns "restaurants might have a double entry problem" into a plan. That requires knowing what a restaurant is, what the problem means, and what a sensible first move would be.

It is also the piece most likely to disappoint even when built. Generated steps read well and are often not what you would actually do. Worth going in with that expectation.

Asking what got done

No AI needed

"It asks you at the end of the day what tasks you have completed."

That is step 6, already in your plan. The bot asks in the evening, you answer in your own words, your answer is saved exactly as typed.

Rolling unfinished work forward

No AI needed

"If you haven't completed these tasks, if they are important, they will be rolled over to the next day's tasks."

Straightforward, as long as you mark what is important rather than asking the app to guess. Your database already has a postpone_count column, so it can also tell you what you keep putting off, which is often the more useful thing.

Building the daily list

HalfHalf

"It puts tasks on the daily to do list for you based off your goals and how far along you are in your journey."

Ordering what is already there: yes, and better than AI would. Your database knows your ultimate goal, your projects, and exactly which roadmap step you are on. Work serving the step you are on now outranks work that does not. That is a fact, not an opinion, and you can correct it by moving the step.

Inventing tasks you have not thought of: needs AI.

The daily briefing, and the traps

HalfHalf

"A daily briefing of things to do and things not to do that you usually fall into the trap of doing that misguide you from your path."

You are already doing this and it works. Your six trading rules are precisely a list of traps you fall into, written by you, read back every morning. "Don't look at P&L" is that exact thing. The same pattern extends to the rest of your life with no AI whatsoever: you write the traps, the app puts them in front of you daily.

Spotting traps you have not noticed yourself: needs AI. That means watching your behaviour over months and finding a pattern.

The honest recommendation

Build everything that needs no AI first. Not as a stopgap. Because for deciding what matters to you today, something you can check beats something that sounds confident.
1You get a working system in weeks, not monthsEvery no-AI piece is ordinary building. Nothing experimental, nothing that might not pan out.
2You find out what you actually wantRight now the AI half is a guess about your own future behaviour. After a month of real use it stops being a guess.
3The AI version is still there afterwardsYour database already has is_private and ai_processed waiting. Switching it on later is a small change, not a rebuild. Your brief planned it that way.
4Nothing you build now is wastedThe AI version reads the same tables, the same goals, the same roadmap. It sits on top rather than replacing.

What it will and will not be

So there is no ambiguity later.
